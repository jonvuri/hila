Phase 10 · Session 4 · Deep dive — companion to Theme 01

Wipeout × Overlaid Cards

Six concepts for one design goal: Miller columns that physically carry their deep hierarchy — every collapsed level visible, grabbable, and cheap — using only the theme's legal moves: low-poly geometry, negative space, and typography. All six render the same scene: hila › Research › Reading queue › [DDIA] › Part I › [Ch. 3 — active] (two open panels, four collapsed levels). The 7b mechanics under all of them are unchanged — universal tab layer, gap-ancestor cards, per-depth steps — only the physical expression varies.

Judge on  px cost per collapsed level ·  depth readable at a glance, not by counting ·  click/grab affordance without added chrome ·  survives 8+ levels deep ·  accent stays focus/live only
OC-AInstrument strip, column-locked

The catalog baseline, refined: the tab strip is broken into per-column groups, each flush with its column's left edge, staircasing within the group. The negative space between groups is what tells you where one column's ancestry ends and the next begins — alignment does the work a border would. Three grades of card chrome below, from full edges down to bare ticks.

Both edge directions survive, but only as short, unconnected ticks. Every tab throws a 10px tick from its bottom-left corner, and the matching ancestor card's top tick starts on that same invisible horizontal line — tab and card are tied together by alignment across the gap, not by any drawn connector. (Where a tab sits directly on its own card's edge — HILA, PART I — the two ticks meet into an open corner for free.) Ancestor edges are now a single top tick; only panels keep bottom ticks, so "has a floor" quietly comes to mean "is open". A panel corner is an H tick and a V tick that never meet — the corner exists in the negative space between them (the CornerNotchBox move, exploded) — and the active panel earns four open corners in accent, re-deriving the catalog's bracket language from first principles.

OC-BThe depth gauge

Hierarchy as an instrument readout: a fixed rail on the left renders the entire chain as a vertical ladder — every level one cell, lit progressively, with unlit ghost cells admitting the depth that could exist. Columns stop carrying their own ancestry entirely; they carry only a level index that points back at the gauge. Collapsed levels cost zero horizontal space in the column field.

OC-CEdge-on sheets

The card metaphor made low-poly-literal: a collapsed level is the same sheet as an open panel, just rotated edge-on — a flat parallelogram sliver, leaning toward focus. Opening a level is (conceptually) rotating its sheet face-on. One geometric rule carries the whole hierarchy; no tabs, no fades.

OC-DSpines

Typography as the physical object: a collapsed level is a book spine — a full-height slat with its title running vertically in the display voice — and an open panel is the book laid open beside it. The stream becomes a shelf: spine, spine, spine, page, spine, page. Nothing here is chrome; every pixel of structure is a word.

OC-EChevron flow

The racing line: ancestry as a single strip of interlocked chevron segments, every segment pointing at focus. Panels are plain columns whose top rules the strip "lands on" — the accent segment pours directly into the active panel's rule. Depth compresses gracefully: any run of ancestors can squeeze to a bare »n segment that re-expands on hover.

OC-FThe terrace

The zero-chrome answer: each column heads itself with its ancestry set as a descending terrace of micro-labels — one line per level, each indented one step and one shade brighter — landing on the column's own title in display type. Depth is drawn with nothing but type, indentation, and the negative space between columns. There is not a single border in this mock. Its weakness is the vertical bill — one full line per level — which the two variants below attack while staying almost chrome-free.

The terrace pushes the column's content right by its total depth — the indentation is honest: deeper columns really do start further in, so the two columns' content edges themselves form a staircase. Each terrace line is a click target (it's an ancestor tab with no costume). The risk is verticality: at depth 8 the terrace is 8 lines tall — likely wants the »n squeeze from OC-E above some threshold.

F1's vertical stack rotated back onto one line: ancestors run inline, and depth is carried by the same two cues the original tab staircase used — each segment's baseline steps down 3px and its ink steps brighter toward focus. Separation is a wide word-gap, nothing else. Cost: one line of micro-type per column, at any depth (a depth-9 run wraps or takes the »n squeeze). This is the closest typographic transliteration of the original design's staggered tabs — the stagger survives, the borders don't. The title keeps a single fixed 12px indent under the run (the cumulative-indent honesty of F1 is the trade given up).

The terrace rotated into the left gutter: ancestors stack right-aligned beside the title block, spending the header's dead horizontal space instead of any vertical lines — the column grows by zero pixels regardless of depth (until the stack outgrows the header zone, then »n). Right-alignment makes every run end flush against the title's edge — the ragged left is the staircase now, pointing at what it leads to. This is also the surfaces "stellation" idea grown up: structure annotated in the margin, content untouched. Chrome budget: still zero borders; the one thing it costs is the reserved 132px gutter — negative space doing the work, but reserved negative space.
